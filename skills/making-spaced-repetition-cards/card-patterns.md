# Card patterns

One pattern per kind of material, each with a good example. Then four real cards Rufus rejected, with rewrites. Answers aim for 15 words or fewer; 25 is the hard limit. Every answer must be clear enough for an AI grader to score a reply against it without doubt.

## Patterns

| Material | Front | Back | type tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term | What is a holon, in Wilber’s sense? | A whole that is also a part of a larger whole. | concept |
| Term, built on it | What does Wilber call a ranking of holons in which each level includes the ones below? | A holarchy. | concept |
| Contrast | How does a holarchy differ from a dominator hierarchy? | A holarchy includes its lower levels; a dominator hierarchy oppresses them. | contrast |
| Argument | Why does Wilber rank life above matter, though matter is far bigger? | Life contains matter as a part; matter doesn’t need life. | why |
| Connection | What mistake does Wilber’s “destroy a level” test guard against? | Ranking levels by size rather than by what contains what. | why |
| Example | What is an example of a holon from biology? | A cell: whole in itself, part of an organ. | concept |
| Names of a structure | What are Wilber’s four quadrants? | Upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right. | list |
| One part | Which of Wilber’s four quadrants covers an individual’s inner experience? | Upper-left. | concept |
| Long list, one missing | The five aggregates: form, feelings, perceptions, mental formations, and …? | Consciousness. | list |
| True/false | True or false (give the reason): Wilber ranks matter above life because matter is bigger. | False: life is higher because it contains matter. | tf |
| Multiple choice | Which does Wilber call a holarchy? (a) a ranking where higher levels dominate lower ones (b) a ranking where higher levels include lower ones (c) no ranking at all | (b) | mc |
| Fill the blank | For Wilber, a whole that is also a part is called a ____. | holon | cloze |
| Quote, wording | The Dhammapada opens with what comes before all things. Complete: “Phenomena are preceded by the heart, …” | ruled by the heart, made of the heart. | quote |
| Verse line | Dhammapada: “Hostilities aren’t stilled through hostility, regardless.” (next line?) | Hostilities are stilled through non-hostility: | line |
| Reflection | When did a thought you kept feeding today shape what you did? | No fixed answer. Pass if you brought a real moment to mind. | reflect |

Notes on the harder kinds:

- **Contestable claims.** Put the author on the front ("In Wilber's view …", "for Thich Nhat Hanh …"). If you think a claim is weak or wrong, drop it rather than card it.
- **Quotes and lines.** Write the quote as one run of text, without " / " for line breaks, so the checker can match it. Give a context cue plus the opening words; never only a topic ("What did the Buddha say about X?" has no single right answer). Keep the quote short: one or two lines.
- **Memorising a whole poem or passage.** Line cards come after the reader has learned the text by reading it aloud. Show one or two full lines, enough that only one continuation fits, and ask for the next. A two-word fragment like "Deep, slow" is too thin a cue. Make one card per line, all with the same deck tag, so they can be studied together. Use this only when the reader's goal includes the wording.
- **Reflection prompts.** These are optional and suit practice texts. Grade on whether you engaged, not on a right answer. Make no more than one per key teaching, tag them `type::reflect` so the reader can suspend them, and leave them out of the core deck unless asked.
- **True/false.** Use it for a likely misreading or a reversed claim, never for a trivially true statement. Write it so a reader who doesn't know couldn't guess, and mix true and false statements in roughly equal numbers. The front says "give the reason", and the back starts with "True:" or "False:" followed by the reason. A grader scores 10 for the right verdict with the reason, and 5 or less for the verdict alone.
- **Multiple choice.** Options are real look-alikes from the text, not filler. Keep it to three or four, with one right answer.
- **Lists whose items aren't concepts** (say, four things Marcus tells himself to remember) skip the names-first card. Use one-missing-item cards or one card per item that matters.
- **Fill the blank.** Blank one key term or phrase, with `____`. A blank inside a quotation is fine; the checker treats it as a wildcard. This works on Basic cards, so there's no Cloze note type to set up. Don't blank words a reader could guess from grammar.
- **Why cards.** Ask for the reason the text gives, framed so it's the only fair answer: name the test, the example or the mechanism. The answer is a reason clause, not a summary of the argument: "A real whole needs a unifying level above its parts", not three sentences of Wilber.

## Four rejected cards, rewritten

Rufus reviewed these from the *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* deck in AnkiMobile (2026-11-22). His words are in quotes.

**1. Two things, subtle answer.** Front: "What is the difference between 'differentiation' going wrong (dissociation) and 'transcendence' going wrong (repression), in Wilber's scheme?" The back ran to 50 words. "Not obvious what answer should be and quite subtle. Asking 2 things at once."
Rewrite as two cards:
- In Wilber's view, what goes wrong when new parts emerge but are never integrated? → Dissociation.
- In Wilber's view, what goes wrong when a higher level denies the lower instead of including it? → Repression.

**2. Structure asked whole.** Front: "What are Wilber's Four Quadrants, and what does each one study?" The back was four sentences. "Could have been 5 cards eg what are 4 quadrants and one question for each of them."
Rewrite: the "Names of a structure" card above, then one card per quadrant, like "One part". A card that defines what each name means comes before both.

**3. Verbose, doubtful, low value.** Front: "Why does Wilber say you don't need to be a genius to move into vision-logic, using the analogy of a child not needing to be Shakespeare…?" "One answer could have just been: you don't need to master a stage. But worse is this even really the case… I also just wonder if this is really worth a card."
Fix: drop it. The claim is doubtful and minor, and the front gives the answer away. If the stage idea matters to the reader, card the general point instead:
- In Wilber's view, must you master a developmental stage before moving beyond it? → No: adequate competence is enough.

**4. Undefined terms, not central, repeated.** Front: "Why does Wilber say 'the bios is not a part of the cosmos, but just the opposite'… even though the physical cosmos has far more span?" "What is cosmos/physiosphere anyway?… is this a key part of SES?? And we have the same holon hierarchy question multiple times. The main point is hierarchy point which could be asked more simply with right or wrong answer much more obvious."
Rewrite as one card, with no undefined terms, and cut the other holon-hierarchy repeats:
- Why does Wilber rank life above matter, though matter is far bigger? → Life contains matter as a part; matter doesn't need life.
